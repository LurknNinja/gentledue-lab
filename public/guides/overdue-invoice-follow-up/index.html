How to follow up on an overdue invoice without sounding hostile

You did the work. Now you need to ask about payment. These examples keep the request clear, respectful, and easy to act on.

Before you send a reminder, verify that the invoice is actually overdue, the amount is correct, and the payment has not already been made. Check the original payment terms and give the client an easy way to confirm the status.

1. A gentle first reminder

Subject: Checking in on invoice [number] Hi [name], I'm following up on invoice [number] for [amount], due [date]. Could you let me know whether payment is already in progress? If it would help, I'm happy to resend the invoice. Thanks, [your name]

Use this when a payment is only recently overdue and you want to leave room for an ordinary administrative delay.

2. A direct second reminder

Subject: Follow-up: invoice [number] is still outstanding Hi [name], I wanted to follow up on invoice [number] for [amount]. I haven't seen the payment come through yet. Could you confirm the expected payment date or let me know if there's a question about the invoice? Thanks for the update, [your name]

Use a second reminder if you've given the recipient time to respond. Avoid implying they have ignored your message if you cannot verify delivery.

3. If the client says something is wrong

Subject: Re: invoice [number] Hi [name], Thanks for flagging the concern. Could you tell me which line item or part of the work you're questioning? I'll review that against our agreement and get back to you with a clear answer. Best, [your name]

Don't threaten collections, penalties, or legal steps in a template. These depend on your contract, location, and circumstances.

4. Keep a simple follow-up record

Record the invoice number, due date, last contact, any promised payment date, and whether the invoice is disputed. If you have a professional relationship to preserve, consistency helps more than anger.
